Our promise

If we ever close.

A family archive is meant to last longer than any company. We hope The Cultured Human runs for a very long time, but you should know now, not on the day, what would happen to your family's voices if it ever stops.

Last updated 20 November 2026.

The short version

  • You can take a complete copy of your archive at any time, today, with one button. It doesn't depend on us still being here.
  • If we decide to close, you will hear from us at least 90 days before, and everything keeps working while you take your copy.
  • Your archive is never sold. It can only go to someone who keeps this promise, and you can delete it first.
  • Anyone who takes it on must also keep our promise about voices: no cloned voices, and no recordings used to train AI.
  • When we close, we delete everything, and tell you when it is done.

Your copy, any day

In Settings, Download everything gives you one zip file with every story, photograph, recording and document you have added, each as an ordinary file with a readable name. Inside is a page called Start here that opens in any browser, without the internet and without an account, and shows your archive the way you kept it. There is also archive.json, which holds everything again in an open format another service or a technical family member can read.

The copy is free, on every plan, forever. We will never put it behind a payment.

If we decide to close

  1. At least 90 days' notice. We email everyone with an account, and every legacy contact, and put a notice at the top of the site and the archive. We send reminders 30 days and 7 days before the end.
  2. Everything keeps working. You can still sign in, read, and download your copy until the last day. We stop taking new sign-ups and new payments.
  3. Money back. Any paid plan is refunded for the time it would have had left. Bookings with makers, hosts and storytellers that have not taken place are cancelled and refunded in full.
  4. Help if you need it. If downloading is hard for you, write to us and a person will help, or send the copy to someone you name.

Archives that have been passed on, and letters for later

If someone has died and their archive has passed to the people they chose, we email those people the same notice, so they can take their copy too.

A letter you have set to open on a future date stays in your own copy, marked with that date. We will write to ask whether you want us to send it early. If its writer can no longer answer, we send it to the person it is for before we close, with the date the writer chose on the front.

Nothing is sold

We will never sell your archive, or anything in it, as data. If another organisation offers to keep the service running, your archive can move to them only if they publicly take on this promise and our privacy policy as they stand. We will tell you who they are before anything moves, and you can delete your archive, or ask us not to move it, first.

After the last day

We delete every archive, every file, and every copy in our backups within 30 days of closing, and email to confirm it is done. What you shared publicly with The Commons is deleted too, unless you asked us to give it to an archive or library that will keep it public.

Changing this promise

We can only make this promise stronger. If we ever had to change it in any other way, we would email you first, and you would have at least 90 days to take your copy before the change applied to you.
