Privacy

What we keep, and why.

Your family's stories are the most personal thing you can trust anyone with. This page says, in plain words, what we keep, who can see it, and how you take it back.

Last updated 18 November 2026.

The short version

  • We keep only what the site needs to work. We don't sell it, we don't show ads, and we don't use advertising trackers.
  • Your family archive is private by default. You choose who sees each entry.
  • What you share with The Commons is public only after a person has read it, and only with the credit you chose.
  • You can change or delete what you shared, and delete your whole account, at any time.

What we keep

When you sign in

Your email address, so we can send you a sign-in link, and the name you choose to show. There is no password. If you sign in with Google, Google tells us your email address and name.

Your family archive

The stories, letters, voice notes, videos, photographs and documents you add, the people you share them with (their name, email address and how they are related to you), the people you name as legacy contacts, and your roots portrait if you choose to keep it in your archive. If you set up the weekly question for an elder, we keep their name and WhatsApp number to send it, and any answer they send back to our number goes into your archive, kept as Only me.

What you share with The Commons

What you write, the place it comes from, the credit you chose, and whether it answers a week's question. If a moderator doesn't publish something, we keep their reason so you can see it. We also keep when you say "We have this too" (others only see a count) and any reports you send (the person reported is never told who reported them).

Makers, hosts and storytellers, and the families who book them

Listings, requests, quotes and replies, and the email addresses of both sides. Each side sees the other's email address only after the family accepts a quote. Payments are taken by a payment provider; we never see or keep card details, only a payment reference.

On your device

The site keeps a few things in your browser: your sign-in, your choice of light or dark theme, and, until you sign in, anything you try out in preview mode. The roots portrait you draw on Trace your roots stays in your browser unless you choose to keep it in your archive. We don't use cookies for tracking.

Who can see it

  • The people you choose. Each archive entry has its own audience: only you, your family, specific people, specific people from a date, or the people you leave it to. "Only me" stays private forever.
  • Moderators read what is sent to The Commons before it is published, and makers', hosts' and storytellers' listings before they go up. They also see bookings, to settle payments. They cannot read your private archive.
  • Everyone can read contributions after they are published, with the credit you chose, and published listings.

Who helps us run it

We use a few services to run the site. Each one handles your information only to provide that service to us:

  • Supabase stores the database and your files, and sends sign-in emails.
  • Vercel hosts the website.
  • Resend sends emails such as "Your contribution is published" or "You have a new request".
  • Google Fonts serves the site's typefaces, so your browser contacts Google when a page loads.
  • WhatsApp (Meta) delivers the weekly question, and the answers sent back to it, only if you set it up for an elder.
  • A payment provider takes payments for bookings.
  • OpenAI writes down and translates a voice note or video, only when someone who can edit that entry presses "Transcribe and translate it". The recording is sent for that one job. OpenAI's terms for its API say it does not use what is sent to train its models. You can always type a transcript yourself instead, and nothing is sent.

If we turn on visitor counts, we will use Vercel Web Analytics, which counts visits without cookies and without identifying you. It may also count a few moments on public pages, such as whether a first story was kept on the home page or its card shared, but never what you wrote or recorded.

Emails

We email you only about things you are waiting for: sign-in links, what happened to something you shared, and the steps of a booking. We don't send marketing emails.

Taking it back

  • Change or delete anything you shared with The Commons from what you have shared.
  • Delete your whole account from Settings. This deletes your archive and its files, what you added to other people's archives, what you shared with The Commons, your listings and your bookings.
  • To ask what we hold about you, to correct it, or anything else about your information, message us on Instagram.

Children

The site is for adults. If you are writing about a child in your family, please keep it within your family archive rather than The Commons.

Changes

If we change how we use your information, we will update this page and the date at the top. If the change is significant, we will email you.
