A thought to carry

A thought to carry

Maha Upanishad 6.71–73

“Vasudhaiva kutumbakam” — the world is one family.

Where it comes from

The phrase closes a verse in the Maha Upanishad, which contrasts the narrow-minded, who sort people into “mine” and “not mine”, with the generous, for whom the whole earth is kin. A version of the same line also appears in the Hitopadesha.

Why it still matters

It is now quoted far beyond its source — in speeches, classrooms and diplomacy. Reading it in context reminds us that it was offered as a description of a generous mind, not a slogan.

Regional spotlight · Gujarat & Rajasthan

Where water became architecture.

In the dry west of India, communities built stepwells — stone stairways descending to groundwater. They were places to draw water, to rest in the cool and to gather. Rani ki Vav in Patan, Gujarat, dates from the eleventh century and became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014. Chand Baori in Abhaneri, Rajasthan, folds hundreds of criss-crossing steps into a single deep well.

Craft story · Telangana

The pattern is dyed before the cloth exists.

In ikat, bundles of yarn are tied and dyed before they go on the loom, so the design only appears as the weaver brings warp and weft together. Pochampally ikat, from the villages around Bhoodan Pochampally, is protected by a Geographical Indication tag — a legal recognition that its name belongs to its place and its weavers.
