My Roots

Trace where you come from. Keep what your family knows.

Most of us live far from where our families began. The places fade first, then the stories that went with them. My Roots brings both together: a portrait of the places your family has called home, and a private archive for the voices, sayings and memories behind them.

Trace your roots, free Open your archive

Tracing needs no account. The archive is free to start and private by default.

01 · Trace

The places your family has called home.

Add where you live, then where your parents, grandparents and those before them lived, and watch the portrait grow.

  • Your mother's and your father's side, up to six generations
  • Five styles: roots, a tree, a river, rings or stars
  • Words on the portrait in English or Hindi
  • Download it, print it as a poster, or ask a relative to fill in the gaps
Trace your roots

02 · Keep

What your family knows, kept for those who come next.

A private archive for the stories behind the places, in your family's own words and voices.

  • Stories, voice notes, videos, photographs, sayings and documents
  • One question a week to an elder, answered in a voice note
  • You choose who sees each entry, now or on a date
  • Passed on only when you decide, to the people you chose
How the archive works

How they fit together

From a first place to a family's memory.

  1. Trace

    Draw the places your family has lived. It stays in your browser until you choose to keep it. Start now.

  2. Keep

    Save the portrait in your archive and open it on any device. Add the stories that go with each place.

  3. Ask

    Send an elder one question a week and keep their voice-note replies, before the answers are gone.

  4. Share and return

    Add what your family carries to your place's page in The Commons, and go back with a local host on a journey.

Ask before it's gone

Twelve questions to ask your elders.

  1. Where were you born, and what did the house look like?
  2. What did your parents do for a living?
  3. Which languages did you speak at home, and which words do you miss?
  4. What did a festival day look like when you were a child?
  5. Which dish takes you straight back home? Who made it best, and how?
  6. Who in the family moved away first, and why?
  7. What did you carry with you when you left home?
  8. Which song or saying do you remember from your own grandparents?
  9. Was there a ritual our family never skipped?
  10. What were you told about our family name, or our village?
  11. What do you wish your grandchildren knew about where we come from?
  12. What do you miss most, and what are you glad has changed?

Tips for the conversation

  • Ask first if you may record, then use your phone's voice recorder, or record straight into your archive.
  • Let silences sit. The best stories often come after a pause.
  • Ask for photographs and objects. They unlock memories.
  • Write down names and places, and check the spellings.
