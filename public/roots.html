Your roots · Begin here

Everyone comes from somewhere.

Most of us live far from where our families began: another city, another country, another language. The recipes, songs, sayings and places our elders carry don't disappear all at once. They fade quietly, when nobody asks. Here is how to start asking.

Trace your roots Questions to ask your elders

Four ways back

From a first question to a journey home.

  1. Trace

    Map the places your family has lived, generation by generation. Start now.

  2. Ask

    Sit with your elders and ask the questions below, before the answers are gone.

  3. Keep

    Send an elder one question a week and keep their voice-note replies in your family archive, for the generations after you.

  4. Return

    Find your family's place, and visit it with a local host. Journeys.

Ask before it's gone

Twelve questions to ask your elders.

  1. Where were you born, and what did the house look like?
  2. What did your parents do for a living?
  3. Which languages did you speak at home, and which words do you miss?
  4. What did a festival day look like when you were a child?
  5. Which dish takes you straight back home? Who made it best, and how?
  6. Who in the family moved away first, and why?
  7. What did you carry with you when you left home?
  8. Which song or saying do you remember from your own grandparents?
  9. Was there a ritual our family never skipped?
  10. What were you told about our family name, or our village?
  11. What do you wish your grandchildren knew about where we come from?
  12. What do you miss most, and what are you glad has changed?

Tips for the conversation

  • Ask first if you may record, then use your phone's voice recorder.
  • Let silences sit. The best stories often come after a pause.
  • Ask for photographs and objects. They unlock memories.
  • Write down names and places, and check the spellings.
