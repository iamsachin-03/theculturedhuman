Living heritage · India's many voices

India, many cultures.

A living exploration of the traditions, languages, arts, foodways, architecture and ideas that shape the Indian experience — diverse, evolving and deeply connected to place.

A way of looking

India is not one story told in many accents. It is many stories, each with its own voice.

An editorial thought, not a historical quotation.

Art & craft

Beauty made by hand.

Explore Madhubani and Gond painting, Pattachitra, Kalamkari, Phad, blue pottery, Channapatna toys, Kutch embroidery, Banarasi weaving, Chikankari and countless local practices. Understand the communities, materials, techniques and changing livelihoods behind each form.

Read: the story of Madhubani painting

Language & literature

Many tongues. Many worlds.

Discover poetry, oral epics, folk tales, regional writing, scripts, proverbs and the ways language carries memory. No single language can stand in for India's literary imagination.

Food & festivals

Culture you can taste and celebrate.

Follow seasonal food, regional kitchens, harvest festivals, community celebrations and rituals. Explore their local meanings without reducing living practices to spectacle.

Read: a plate is a map

Music, dance & performance

Traditions in motion.

From Hindustani and Carnatic traditions to Baul, Bihu, Lavani, Qawwali, folk theatre, Kathakali, Bharatanatyam and contemporary forms — listen for the histories and people behind the performance.

Architecture & place

Stories written in stone, wood and space.

Explore temples, mosques, stepwells, forts, havelis, colonial-era streets, vernacular homes and contemporary architecture through the people and places that give them meaning.

Ideas & ways of life

Plural traditions of thought.

Indian philosophical schools, Buddhist and Jain traditions, Bhakti and Sufi movements, reformers, scientists and modern thinkers — approached with historical context and room for disagreement.

Our editorial approach

Celebrate deeply. Explain carefully.

We avoid treating India as a single, uniform culture. We credit artists and communities, distinguish documented history from legend, and make space for regional differences, living debates and contemporary voices.
