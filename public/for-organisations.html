For organisations

Bring India's living culture to your team.

Sessions led by named artists and practitioners for offsites, festival weeks and onboarding, plus gifting, school programmes and heritage partnerships. Every session starts with a real story and ends with something made, tasted or understood.

Plan a session How we pay practitioners

What a session looks like

Story first, then hands-on.

Each session is hosted by a practitioner whose tradition it is, introduced by name. We begin with the history and the people behind the practice, drawn from the research in our journal, then everyone takes part.

Sessions run online or in person and are shaped around your team: a quiet hour during a busy week, a festival celebration or a welcome for colleagues who are new to India.

Sessions to begin with

Three ways into India.

Art · hands-on

A Madhubani painting hour

A Mithila painter shares how women's wall paintings for weddings became an art known around the world. Then everyone paints a traditional motif of their own to keep.

Food · conversation

A plate is a map

How geography, history and the seasons shape what India eats, told through a few regional meals. In person, it can include a tasting prepared by a local cook.

Ideas · discussion

An introduction to Indian philosophy

The many schools of Indian thought, including the traditions that questioned the Vedas, explained clearly and with room for questions.

How we work with you

Simple, and clear about money.

  1. Tell us about your team

    Size, city or online, the occasion and any dates.

  2. We propose a session

    With the practitioner named, an outline and what everyone will need.

  3. A clear quote

    The practitioner's fee is shown separately from ours, and agreed before anything is booked.

  4. The session, and after

    Everyone leaves with a short reading list from the journal to keep going.

Partnerships

Heritage work that lasts.

If your organisation supports craft, heritage or community projects, we would like to help document them and tell their story well. Where funding needs a registered implementing organisation, we work alongside one.

Our promise

No props, no stereotypes.

Traditions are presented by the people who practise them, with their names, in context. We do not stage culture as a costume.
